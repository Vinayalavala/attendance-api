Indexes. Two unique ones: employees(emp_code) and attendance_logs(emp_code, date); they enforce the business rules and also serve the per-employee monthly query and the summary's lookup. (date desc, emp_code) serves the default list order, the leaderboard and the trend; (status, date desc, emp_code) the status filter; (emp_code, punch_in desc) punch-out; (department, joined_on) headcount. I rejected a partial index on late_minutes > 0 for the leaderboard: a month is small, and it would slow every write.
Punch-in race. Both requests build the same record and call insert_one. The unique (emp_code, date) index lets MongoDB accept one; the other gets DuplicateKeyError, which I return as 409. I never read first.
Ties. Ranking happens in the database with $rank (1, 2, 2, 4) before I apply limit. I keep every row whose rank is within limit, so a tie at the cutoff returns extra rows, ordered by minutes then emp_code.
Headcount. The summary starts from employees (joined on or before month end), then looks up the month's logs and keeps people with none. Headcount is the number of distinct emp_codes per department.
100x data. I would stop aggregating raw logs per request: keep a daily per-employee rollup updated on write, and copy department onto it.
